Ashborn: The Long Road Home

Ironsworn at heart. Rebuilt for destruction.Played for redemption.

A hopeful dark fantasy roleplaying game about coming home from a war you were forced to fight.

The war is over, but the weapons it made are still around.
Will you make it home?

The game

You were made to kill, and nobody made you for peace

The Dark Lord ruled for a thousand years. His agents hunted the best warriors of every kin and snatched them from their homes. They were dragged to the Darklands, drowned in magic pools called the Black Mirrors, and rebuilt as weapons of mass destruction, as Ashborn.

Five years ago he fell. The war was brutal, and it took all the free folk of the world to end him. The world is healing, but the scars of the war are everywhere.

The dark veil has lifted from your mind. But you are still a freak of nature, a monster rebuilt to do the Dark Lord's work. People keep their distance and shut you out until you have proven worthy of their trust.

How it plays

One roll, and a die that grows with every wound

If you know Ironsworn, you already know most of it: the same two Challenge Dice and the same Strong Hit, Weak Hit and Miss. Ashborn drops moves, assets and vows, and puts one growing action die in their place.

One roll to rule them all

Say what you do and what you want from it. Roll your Rage Die, add one of five Abilities, and compare the total with two ten-sided Challenge Dice. Beat both for a Strong Hit, one for a Weak Hit, neither for a Miss. The system is player-facing, so the GM never rolls in combat.

The die that grows

Every point of Harm moves the Rage Die one step up. A bigger die wins more rolls, but it also gets harder to control. If the bare die ever beats both Challenge Dice added together, you go Berserk and attack whatever set you off, without a roll and without choosing the target. At a D4 that happens about once in a hundred rolls, and at a D20 almost every other roll.

Two tracks home

Humanity goes up when you hold back. Standing shows what one town thinks of you. It grows slowly, job by job, and you can lose it in a single bad night. When you think a town is ready, you roll both against the dice and ask to be taken in. If you beat both, you are home, and you choose when to ask.

An evening of Ashborn can go like this. You reach the gate of Rivermoot without a letter, and the guards would rather send a monster after a missing farmer's son than one of their own. You find him in the hills with the men who took him. Your Rage Die is already a D10 from the last fight, and you could end it with one swing. If you let them run instead, you mark Humanity, and Rivermoot hears about it.

You get no experience for killing. You mark Humanity when you hold back, protect someone, make amends or help someone at a cost to yourself. Every level gives you back a memory from before the war, and the first one is your name.

The Ashborn

Five Molds, each with a number where a name used to be

One for each kin the Dark Lord harvested. Each comes with a Gift, a Skill, a weapon, a kit and a Dark Secret from the war. Until Humanity 1, a serial number is the only name you have.

The world

Five lands, and every city keeps its own count of you

The map has five lands. Stonehold in the mountains, the Vale in the valley at the heart of it, the Aeonwoods, the Hearthlands, and the Darklands in the north, where the Black Mirrors still stand. Each land has one central city, built by one of the kin the Dark Lord harvested, and every one of those cities starts you at zero. They remember what the Ashborn did, so you have to earn their trust one job at a time before the gates open for good. The free quickstart runs in the Vale, with Rivermoot as the first town that has to decide what you are. Rivermoot and Silvarion are inked. Würmhall, Applecrust and Gorrath are on Joe's table now.
