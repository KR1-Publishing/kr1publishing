The method

The journey is the session

Most campaigns skip the travel. A hexcrawl plays it. Every day on the road costs something, every hex hides something, and the map fills in as the players push into it.

The series gives the GM procedures for that: the exploration turn, encounter tables that carry meaning, and a region whose history explains why the ruins are where they are. The books are short and built to be used with the map open beside them.

One reviewer on DriveThruRPG put it simply: it cuts straight to the point, and it is built to get you playing with a minimum of up-front work.

Questions

Before you buy

Which game system does it work with?

Any fantasy RPG. The series is system-neutral and written for GMs who run Shadowdark, OSR games, 5e, Pathfinder 2e or something else. The procedures sit on top of the rules you already use.

Do I have to read them in order?

No. Each book stands on its own. Book one is the best place to start if hexcrawling is new to you, because it sets up the exploration turn the other books build on.

What is in the Complete Playkit?

The guidebooks in one bundle. DriveThruRPG only charges you for the books you do not already own, so it works as an upgrade too.

Are the books available in print?

Books one to three are print on demand from DriveThruRPG as softcovers. The Thornwall Frontier is out as PDF, with print to follow. Shops can order at retailer terms on the vendor page.

How was AI used?

The Hexcrawl books are labelled Contains AI-Generated Content on DriveThruRPG, and we want to be open about why. AI tools were used in early drafts and development. The final text, the editing, the layout and the illustrations are done by people. None of the art is AI generated.

Who makes the series?

Matthijs Holter writes the books. Karl Otto Kristoffersen edits and publishes them, Michael Sollien does the layout and Nicolai Strøm paints the covers.
