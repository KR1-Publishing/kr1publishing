The Core Test

Does your game know what it is? The core of a game is two things: a frame, what it is about, and a loop, what the table actually does, round after round. Three questions reveal both in minutes. A game without a core spreads in every direction.

The frame
1
What is the game about?

Set a thematic frame, genre plus theme, and bake the premise into it. One sentence.

Dungeons & Dragons: A fantasy roleplaying game about heroes who grow by overcoming ever greater dangers.
The loop, part one
2
How do the rules express it?

The theme belongs in the rules, not just the illustrations. What do you count, roll or track? At least one core rule should be impossible to explain without the theme.

Dungeons & Dragons: Levels, experience points, and the most detailed subsystem in the book is combat. The rules spend their weight exactly where the sentence says they should.
The loop, part two
3
What behavior does the game reward?

Players do what pays, not what you hoped. What gives growth or XP? What gives resources or status? What gets punished? The incentive is the real rulebook.

Dungeons & Dragons: XP for defeating enemies. You get stronger, so you can face even stronger enemies. The loop closes perfectly: defeat, grow, seek bigger danger.

Now check the seams

The seams are where the frame meets the loop. Read your three answers out loud, then judge each seam honestly. A beautiful frame bolted onto a loop that plays a different game is how projects die at ninety percent.

Seam 1 to 2. Do the rules prove the sentence? Could a stranger guess what your game is about from the mechanics alone?

Seam 2 to 3. Does the reward push players toward that rule? Or does it pay them to ignore it?

Seam 3 to 1. Is what the game rewards the same thing the game is about? Would a table full of strangers act out your premise just by chasing the points?

Write your core loop

Answer 1 is your frame. Answers 2 and 3 together are your loop, the engine of your game. Write the engine out in four beats, each feeding the next, and check that the whole loop still lives inside the frame.

The three questions are Jared Sorensen's, the sharpest design tool ever put in three lines. The seams, the loop and the framing are from my own runs with them, including the five years it took me to find the core of my own first game. A strong brand is not the same as a strong core, and the three questions reveal the difference in seconds.
