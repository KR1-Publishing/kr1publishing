What each band is for

Free or PWYWList builders and tasters. One per product line is plenty. Note that on DriveThruRPG a sale under twenty cents does not count toward a bestseller badge, so free titles rarely earn metals.
$2.99 to $4.99The workhorse band. First purchases, entry points, series installments. This is where a stranger will take a chance on a name they have never seen.
$7.99 to $9.99Flagship PDFs with demand you have already proven. Earned, not assumed. If nothing of yours has sold well yet, this band is a guess.
$10 and upBig books, boxed content, or an established name. Every dollar above ten asks the buyer to trust you more, so the page has to work harder.
PrintPrice print on top of the PDF, never instead of it. Same customer, larger order.

Five rules that hold up

  1. Price the catalog, not the product. Every title has a job. Some exist to introduce you to a stranger, some exist to reward the reader who already trusts you. Judge a price by what it does to the whole shelf rather than to one line in a spreadsheet.
  2. Your first job is to be tried at all. Nobody can love a book they never opened. Whatever band you choose, make sure one thing in your catalog is an easy yes for someone who has never heard your name.
  3. Never run a fire sale below your free tier's dignity. Deep discounts train your readers to wait rather than buy, and the discount you run this month sets the price they expect next month.
  4. A paying customer is a different person from a free downloader. Someone who has paid you once, even a little, behaves nothing like someone who has only ever taken something free. Free builds a list. Paid builds a business.
  5. Raise prices on proof, not on hope. Reviews, repeat buyers and a track record are proof. A long page count and a lot of effort are not, however unfair that feels.
