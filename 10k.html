The story

A drawer full of games and nobody reading them

Karl Otto Kristoffersen published his first game on DriveThruRPG in 2019. Five years later he had sold sixty-nine books, with a day job, two small children and writing time in stolen half hours.

Then he changed the order he did things in. One release in May 2025 sold two hundred and fifty copies in its first week, and by June 2026 the catalog had passed ten thousand books sold.

The book is the working method behind that, written down while it was still fresh. It is short on theory and long on what actually happened, including what failed.

The method

Four phases, and selling comes last

Most new publishers start with the last step. The book walks through the four in the order that worked.

1

Make

One small finished thing, for one real person. A page is enough.

2

Give

Give it away, so people can find you before they have a reason to pay.

3

Grow

Run the loop again. The list you build is what carries the next release.

4

Sell

Sell once the value is obviously bigger than the price.

Questions

Before you buy

Who is it for?

Makers with years of work in the drawer and no audience yet. It is written for small publishers and self-published authors on DriveThruRPG, but the four phases work for any small creative release.

Is this a marketing theory book?

No. It gives you the order things were done in, the flat years included. Every figure comes from the author’s own DriveThruRPG account.

How long is it?

About thirty pages, short enough to read in one evening. It ends with a one-page checklist to print and tick off as you go.

Is it available in print?

Yes. It is a black and white softcover, print on demand from DriveThruRPG, and the PDF is on DriveThruRPG too. Shops can order it on the vendor page.

Was AI used to make it?

I wrote the book myself. AI tools were used as a final proofreader for typos and inconsistencies.

What comes next?

The DTRPG Bestseller Blueprint, the full manual, is planned for 2027. People on the mailing list hear about it first.
