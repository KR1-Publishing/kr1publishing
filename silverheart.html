The game

The silver from the deep shapes both the town and the game

In 1770, Kongsberg was the second largest city in Norway after Bergen. By 1805 the Silver Works, the foundation of the town's existence, had largely shut down. The game is set in that crisis, and students take the roles of poor townspeople burdened with debt, navigating the town's challenges.

The rules are simple, fit on a single sheet, and are about exploring. The goal is that students learn about Kongsberg in the late 1700s, and have fun along the way. Those two things in that order, and it is why it reaches students who otherwise check out of a history lesson.

The book comes with a high resolution, historically accurate map of old Kongsberg, character sheets both digital and printable, digital game tokens and a player's guide for handing out.

How it has been used

A full school day at Skrim lower secondary

On 29 April 2024 Skrim gave Silverheart a whole day. Three classes, four periods, and the students ran the game themselves.

Skrim lower secondary school, students aged 14 to 15
70students involved
10students as game masters
3classes at once
1.5 hof play per group, minimum

Ten students got a training day of their own beforehand and then led their groups through the adventure. The day opened with a fifteen minute joint introduction, then the students built characters and drew or made something for them. After play they wrote an interview with their own character, laid out as a local newspaper from 1796. The students who did not want to play cooked lapskaus stew and flatbread from 1796 recipes and served the whole year group.

Afterwards a parent wrote to me: "Today we had a really good time at school. We played a Kongsberg game about the old days." That is the sentence I measure the programme against.

Curriculum goals

Language arts and social studies, learned through play

Silverheart is used across two subjects, and the students are assessed in both. The programme maps directly onto these goals from the Norwegian curriculum:

  1. Inform, narrate, argue and reflect in a range of spoken and written genres, for different purposes, adapted to audience and medium.
  2. Explore and describe ways of life, traditions and geography in an area, and see the connections with your own background.
  3. Use sources critically and accountably.

Teachers get started through a workshop. The first ran a full day in February 2024 with ten teachers from the lower secondary schools in Kongsberg, and everyone went home with a class set. The municipality, the Norwegian Mining Museum and the local paper were present.
